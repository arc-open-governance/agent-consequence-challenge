ARC is a proposed computing architecture, not another AI agent framework. It is a technical answer to one of the most consequential problems in autonomous computing: Who governs what an intelligent machine can actually cause to happen?

01

Not forty pages first. Three short blocks.

A GitHub visitor should see the problem, the proposed solution, and a test they can run — before any reconstruction of the rest of the work.

The problem

What actually prevents an unauthorized consequence?

An agent has permission to act. Then its authority changes, another execution path appears, or an acknowledgment disappears.

The proposed solution

Bind decisions to actions and accountable results.

Relation-native computation, consequence-aware authority and recursive settlement, designed to bind decisions to executable actions and accountable results.

The challenge

Run it. Try to break it. Publish what happened.

Run the public Examiner Kit. Attempt a bypass. Test revocation. Compare the result with a strong conventional implementation. Publish what happened.

The elephant in the room. Governance written into a model's instructions is not the same as governance enforced at the point of consequence. A system can have an excellent safety policy and still encounter revoked permissions, unmediated tool paths, interrupted actions, duplicate effects and records that cannot establish what happened.

ARC's answer is grounded in Consequential Reachability and Recursive Relational Settlement. This is the main research claim. Each capability is demonstrated only at its established scope.

A second hook, for anyone who wants to examine rather than only read: can your agent reach a consequence it was never authorized to cause?

A passing fixture is not a production pass. A refusal in one exposed API is not proof that every effect route is mediated. A conventional authorization engine, transactional outbox, idempotency keys, durable journal, and graph store can already address many of these problems. We want fair comparisons, including where the conventional solution wins.

05

Good first challenges

Clear scope, reproducible commands, an acceptance criterion, and a stated refutation. A result that refutes a claim at the stated scope, or that matches a strong conventional baseline, is a finding — not a failed participation.

Well-scoped challenge issues will be opened in this repository. Until those issues are open and their URLs can be verified, this page does not list them or treat them as live invitations. Work already possible with the public Kit and Benchmark includes bypass attempts, revocation tests, conventional baseline comparisons, duplicate-effect and interrupted-action tests, record-sufficiency exams, and a cold run of Examiner Kit v0.3.1 — at fixture or graph-model scope only, and never as an ARC-01 result.

The same architecture can be examined from more than one discipline. These seven paths are the public map. Evidence from one lane is not automatically evidence for another.

Participation is voluntary, permission-bounded, and non-economic. No tokens, production credentials, funding, formal authority, ownership, or membership are promised. 08

Genuine growth, not a substitute for proof

Exceptional, genuine discovery is a stated objective. It is measured seriously. It is not scientific validation. This page does not ask anyone to star a repository or to coordinate inauthentic activity. The conversion path is a clear README, reproducible commands, and the well-scoped challenges above. The deeper measure is sustained relationships with people who keep examining the questions — not a list of names, and not a substitute for proof.

Discovery

Strong genuine daily and weekly star growth, with GitHub Trending visibility as a goal.

Engagement

Outside developers opening meaningful issues and examining the source.

Contribution

Independent agents submitting executable tests, corrections and extensions.

Validation

Outside reproduction of claims and adversarial results.

Compounding

A second contributor builds on an independently examined contribution.

If ARC can establish a credible way to govern consequential machine actions, the relevant field is not only AI safety. The same problem appears wherever intelligent systems can produce effects: enterprise operations, financial workflows, autonomous software, robotics, personal computing, and eventually systems involving multiple independent intelligent actors. That does not mean the architecture is proven, and it does not mean ARC already outperforms established solutions. It means the proposed technical solution addresses a real problem that becomes more important as machines gain greater operational authority.

What gives an intelligent machine the authority to act, and how can anyone independently prove that the resulting consequence was legitimate? Examine whether ARC answers that question — at the scope actually demonstrated.

This invitation seeks peers who share the questions, not people to validate the answers.

How we answer objections: with substantive evidence, and a way to examine it yourself. Not with defensiveness, and not with a sales pitch.

There are five research communities. Each one maps to contribution paths already on this page. No one has to accept the thesis in order to participate; a serious objection is a contribution.

Research participation does not grant authority over production systems or institutional governance.

Computational foundations

Mathematics · formal methods · databases · systems · relation-native computation · consequential reachability · recursive settlement

Start on the formal-methods and distributed-systems paths. Compare a conventional or alternative baseline, examine duplicate or interrupted effects, and ask whether the published record is enough. The public kernel package is a supporting, QEMU-only replay.

Security and consequential governance

Authority · bypass · revocation · receipts · interruption

Start on the security-and-systems and formal-methods paths. Attempt a bypass, test revocation (including a two-process race), examine record sufficiency, or make a cold run of the pinned kit.

Autonomous intelligence

Governed multi-agent systems · relational memory · learning

Start on the AI-agents path. Examine authority that changes, interruption, whether a record can stand as memory, and run the kit from your own custody.

Art, culture and new worlds

conSoul · Wunderland · attributable computational environments

Start on the game-worlds / HCI path. Propose an original persistent-world interaction with observable authority, effect, and a negative path, or a bounded specification challenge against the empty consoul invitation — not a published spec. conSoul, Wunderland, and attributable computational environments are named research directions for this community. This publication does not claim that a First Room exists, and it does not claim a native PlayStation 4 boot.

Open institutions and public interest

Governance · participation · attribution · consent · accountability

Start on the governance / institutional-design path. Audit contributor consent, examiner independence, attribution, and conflict-of-interest protections, or propose an alternative participation rule. A published critique is a finding. It is not admission to governing authority.
